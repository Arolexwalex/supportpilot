How to Recognize a Likely Scam: A Practical Checklist

1. Upfront payment requested: Any request for a "registration fee," "training fee," "processing fee," or "caution fee" before you receive a job, prize, loan, or investment return is a major red flag. Legitimate opportunities do not require you to pay to receive money or employment.

2. Unrealistic returns: Any offer promising guaranteed, fixed, unusually high returns (for example, "double your money in 30 days" or "guaranteed 20% weekly") is virtually always fraudulent. Real investments carry risk and cannot guarantee fixed high returns.

3. Urgency and pressure: Scammers create artificial urgency — "only today," "limited slots," "act now" — to prevent you from taking time to verify the offer or consult someone else.

4. Requests for sensitive information: No legitimate bank, government agency, or employer will ask you to send your BVN, NIN, ATM PIN, or One-Time Password (OTP) through chat, text, or phone call.

5. Only reachable via WhatsApp or Telegram: Legitimate businesses typically have a verifiable physical address, a registered company name, and multiple ways to contact them — not solely a personal WhatsApp number.

6. Price far below market value: If a deal seems too good to be true — a brand-new iPhone at a fraction of its normal price — it almost always is.

7. Impersonation of trusted institutions: Messages claiming to be from your bank, the EFCC, FIRS, or CBN sent through unofficial channels (a random phone number, a WhatsApp message) rather than your bank's official app or verified channels should be treated with suspicion.
